How to play Route 81

Route 81 is a logic grid. Nine rules run across the top, nine run down the side, and every one of the 81 squares needs a Pokémon that fits both its row rule and its column rule. You can only use each Pokémon once per board.

The basics

  1. Read the two rules for a square. A square under "Fire" in a row labelled "Kanto" needs a Fire-type Pokémon first introduced in Kanto, like Charmander or Vulpix.
  2. Tap the square and type a name. Suggestions appear as you type; pick one and confirm.
  3. Right answers lock in. The square shows the Pokémon and, on the Daily board, how many other players picked the same one.
  4. Wrong answers cost a life. You get three. Run out and the board locks, and you can tap any square to see what would have fit.
  5. Hints reveal one valid answer for the square you're on. You get three per board.

The three modes

Daily

One shared board a day, the same for everyone. Every correct pick is added to your Pokédex, and after you finish you can see how rare your answers were compared with everyone else's. Picking an answer few people found is the real challenge.

Draft

Build your own board. Choose the size (anything from 1×1 up to 9×9, rectangles included) and switch whole groups of categories on or off. Want a board of only types and regions? You can deal one. Draft boards are for practice and don't count toward your Pokédex.

Reverse

The grid comes already filled in, and the rules are hidden. Look at the Pokémon in a row or column, work out what they all have in common, and name the category. Any category that fits every Pokémon in the line counts, not only the one we had in mind. The daily Reverse board adds each category you name to your Category Index.

Tips that actually help

  • Start with the tightest squares. A square like "Ultra Beast × Ice" has very few answers. Fill the narrow squares first, before you spend a Pokémon they need somewhere easier.
  • Save flexible Pokémon. A Pokémon that fits lots of categories (a dual type with a common ability, say) is more useful kept in reserve than used on an easy square.
  • Forms count as separate answers. Mega Evolutions, Gigantamax forms and regional forms are their own entries, so Alolan Vulpix and regular Vulpix are two different picks.
  • "Evolved by" means how a Pokémon was reached. "Evolved by Trade" wants the Pokémon you get from the trade (Alakazam), not the one you trade away (Kadabra).
  • Moves mean "can learn." Any Pokémon that can learn the move counts, by level, TM or tutor.
  • In Reverse, look for the boring answer first. Types and regions are the most common hidden rules. Check those before reaching for a move or ability.

Evolution (10)

Base Stage
The first stage of an evolution line, the one everything else evolves from.
Middle Stage
Evolves from something and into something else.
Final Form
The last stage of its line; it doesn't evolve any further.
Doesn't Evolve
Has no evolutions at all. It neither evolves nor is evolved from.
Baby Pokémon
A baby pre-evolution added in a later generation, like Pichu or Igglybuff.
Branched Evolution
Belongs to a line that splits into more than one possible evolution, like Eevee's.
Evolved by Level Up
Reached by leveling up its previous stage.
Evolved by Trade
Reached by trading its previous stage.
Evolved by Friendship
Reached once its previous stage has high friendship.
Evolved by Item
Reached with an item: an evolution stone, or an item held during a trade or level-up.
